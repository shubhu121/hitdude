# Storylines

Reference films: MEGA CLAWD 巨神 (82 s, 36 shots) and RYŪ 龍 (76 s, 30 shots), github.com/seeconever.
Both are three.js toon-shaded scenes pushed through a paint/ink post pipeline. Their own prompt says
"must not look like 3D"; the frames still read as low-poly 3D with a filter. Both tell the same film:
a cute mascot, a summer day, a giant thing wakes, flight, back home at dusk, brush end card.

What we take: a pure function of time, 24 fps, characters on twos, FX on threes, a colour script, a bookend.
What we do differently: no 3D at all, one medium with rules (ink on paper), a design idea the medium itself
carries, and the vocabulary of a AAA game reveal trailer (boss card, impact frames, a title that is earned).

## 1. HITOFUDE 一筆 — "One Stroke"  (picked)

Device: the object is the explanation — the film is an ink painting, the hero's sword is a brush, the enemy is
blank paper. Form: one frame that rebuilds; every big move is the painting being painted or un-painted.
A reveal trailer for a game that does not exist. A swordswoman whose blade is a calligraphy brush fights
THE BLANK, a paper dragon that erases the painted world. She paints a one-stroke dragon (hitofude-ryū, a real
Japanese tradition), the two dragons resolve into a yin-yang, and her last cut is the kanji 一 — which is also
the logo, and the moment colour returns to the world.

- First two seconds: a mountain range is being brushed onto washi, wet ink still spreading, a red sun stamped in.
- Beats: painting paints itself → the girl on the cliff → birds get erased → THE BLANK (boss card) → her eye →
  run → leap → first cut → the world is wiped white → one stroke becomes a dragon → yin-yang → silence →
  一 → colour floods back → title.
- Look: washi ground, sumi ink, one vermilion (her scarf, the sun, the seal). Full colour appears once, at the payoff.
- Sound: wind and near silence, odaiko hits on the cuts, an accelerating drum roll in the fight, dead silence
  before the stroke, a koto phrase when colour returns.

## 2. LAST TRAIN TO ORBIT

Device: a moment in someone's day. Form: cut on the beat. A schoolgirl on a rooftop at dusk sees a comet split;
hangar boot-up in Evangelion-style type cards, catapult launch, one sword draw in the stratosphere, the halves
become shooting stars over the city. Shinkai skies, magenta and teal. Strong, but it is the reference's own
film (mecha stops falling rock) with a better grade.

## 3. MARGIN (the bold one)

Device: the result first. Form: an endless zoom. A flip-book doodle in the margin of a maths notebook: a paper
plane becomes a fighter becomes a mecha, the camera dives through the page into full colour and back out as the
school bell rings. Pencil line boil throughout. Charming, but its payoff is a joke, not a spectacle.
